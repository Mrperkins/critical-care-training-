A new headache during DKA treatment
Case conference. A new headache during DKA treatment. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 7-year-old child, measured weight 22 kilograms, is three hours into treatment for severe DKA. The original presentation included marked dehydration and acidosis. During transfer preparation the child develops headache, vomiting and irritability, then becomes drowsy. Glucose has been falling, which initially distracts the team from the neurological change.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The child opens eyes to voice, has a new decline in interaction and a heart rate that is falling despite continued illness. Pupils are equal. No sedative has been given.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 82, down from 132. Blood pressure is now 128 over 82. Respiratory rate is 28 per minute. Oxygen saturation is 99 percent on room air. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Glucose is 290 milligrams per deciliter. Venous pH is 7.18. Measured sodium is 140 millimoles per liter. Potassium is 3.6 millimoles per liter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. Suspected DKA-associated cerebral injury is a clinical emergency. Equal pupils and a reasonable glucose do not exclude it. Treatment should not wait for confirmatory cranial imaging.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Which change matters more here: the falling glucose or the new neurological deterioration? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. The new headache, vomiting, change in behavior and relative slowing of heart rate raise concern for cerebral injury. Hypoglycemia and other causes still require immediate assessment, but do not delay syndrome-directed treatment. Priorities. 1. Call pediatric critical care and begin the protocol for suspected cerebral injury, including indicated hyperosmolar therapy. 2. Reassess airway protection, perfusion and glucose immediately; avoid unnecessary interruptions in care for imaging. 3. If intubation is unavoidable, use expert planning to preserve appropriate compensation and avoid an abrupt rise in carbon dioxide.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The team treats suspected cerebral injury and transfers with frequent documented neurological checks. A later scan is used to evaluate complications and alternatives after initial resuscitation; it is not the prerequisite for treatment. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
