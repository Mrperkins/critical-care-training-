Blue despite oxygen
Case conference. Blue despite oxygen. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 3-day-old term newborn weighs 3.5 kilograms. The infant has persistent central cyanosis and feeds poorly. Supplemental oxygen produces little change in saturation.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The infant is cyanotic with modest respiratory effort. Breath sounds are equal. The presentation prompts consideration of congenital heart disease, pulmonary hypertension, lung disease and infection.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 164 per minute. Respiratory rate is 54 per minute. Right-hand oxygen saturation is 76 percent on supplemental oxygen. Foot oxygen saturation is 74 percent on supplemental oxygen. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Arterial pH is 7.29. Arterial oxygen tension is 38 millimeters of mercury on the current oxygen support. Lactate is 3.5 millimoles per liter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. Echocardiography is needed urgently to distinguish structural disease and pulmonary hypertension. A small preductal–postductal difference does not rule out a cardiac lesion.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Should you keep increasing oxygen while postponing a cardiac evaluation? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Severe hypoxemia with limited response to oxygen requires a broader circulation-focused assessment. The required balance of pulmonary and systemic blood flow depends on the anatomy. Priorities. 1. Support ventilation and oxygenation while obtaining neonatal cardiac consultation. 2. Discuss prostaglandin and transport planning under the specialist pathway; prepare for apnea if it is used. 3. Use specialist-directed oxygen and ventilation goals rather than imposing one saturation target on every congenital lesion.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. Expert echocardiography establishes the lesion and the stabilization goals. The transport team verifies that infusion, airway and oxygen supplies can sustain the planned journey. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
