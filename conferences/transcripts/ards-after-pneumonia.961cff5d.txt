Oxygenation and lung protection belong together
Gas exchange must be supported while limiting ventilator-induced injury. Predicted body weight, plateau pressure, synchrony and response to PEEP matter; the patient’s actual weight does not define a lung-protective tidal volume. Confirm measured height, predicted-body-weight ventilation and pressure assessment under the ARDS protocol. Reassess PEEP tolerance, hemodynamics and reversible causes of deterioration. Coordinate severe-ARDS strategies, including proning or specialist referral when indicated and operationally feasible. The ventilator plan is a balance of physiological goals. The team needs to know not only the settings but the height, pressure measurements, response and operational limits behind them.

Measure the response to the revised plan
After review of ventilation and support, gas exchange is reassessed with a repeat blood gas and pressure measurements. The team does not assume that a higher saturation means the injured lung has recovered. The receiving unit continues lung-protective ventilation based on predicted rather than actual body weight, evaluates plateau and driving pressures, and uses prolonged prone positioning for severe ARDS when appropriate. Antimicrobial and source-directed care continue. Over the following days in this synthetic course, oxygen requirements decline and the patient eventually separates from invasive ventilation. No isolated improvement in saturation is used to justify injurious tidal volumes or to declare the lung injury resolved. A better saturation is useful evidence, but it does not tell us the injured lung has recovered. Continued pressure and perfusion assessment helps us judge the overall response.
