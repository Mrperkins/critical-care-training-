Now: Total-body depletion with a normal or high serum K.

Diabetic ketoacidosis physiology. Insulin deficiency produces ketogenesis, osmotic diuresis, electrolyte shifts and a high-anion-gap acidosis whose treatment changes potassium and water distribution.

Build the mechanism first. Insulin deficiency and counter-regulatory hormones drive ketogenesis, osmotic diuresis and major total-body electrolyte deficits even when serum levels initially appear normal or high.

At the bedside, track volume status, potassium, sodium/osmolality, anion gap and acid-base trajectory through treatment.

Two traps to actively avoid: Treating glucose normalization as resolution of DKA. And: Forgetting that insulin can rapidly expose profound potassium depletion.

The integration move is this: DKA therapy changes physiology continuously; every lab value belongs to a time point in that transition.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: ketogenesis, anion gap, corrected sodium.

Now: Acid-base and potassium are related but not one-to-one.

Advanced acid-base reasoning. Integrate respiratory and metabolic processes, compensation, anion gap, albumin, chloride and mixed disorders.

Build the mechanism first. pH reflects interacting respiratory and metabolic processes. Compensation has expected patterns, so deviations can expose mixed disorders that a single pH label hides.

At the bedside, calculate the anion gap, correct for relevant confounders, compare compensation and consider chloride, albumin and lactate in context.

Two traps to actively avoid: Calling a normal pH normal acid-base physiology. And: Treating bicarbonate concentration as if it identifies the mechanism by itself.

The integration move is this: Acid-base analysis should lead back to the disease process causing the disturbance.
