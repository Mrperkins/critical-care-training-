Build the mechanism first. Driving pressure is plateau pressure minus total peep and reflects tidal pressure applied to the respiratory system relative to available compliance.

At the bedside, a fixed tidal volume can impose very different stress depending on how much functional lung is available. Trend driving pressure alongside plateau pressure and mechanics.

Two traps to actively avoid: Calculating from peak rather than plateau pressure. And: Treating a threshold as a substitute for understanding lung size and mechanics.

The integration move is this: Connect driving pressure to compliance, tidal volume, recruitability and mechanical power.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: plateau pressure, tidal strain.

The relationship matters because A R D S produces heterogeneous inflammatory lung injury, shunt, edema and a reduced aerated “baby lung,” making regional overdistension possible even at seemingly modest tidal volumes. At the bedside, integrate gas exchange, compliance, recruitability, prone response, driving pressure and hemodynamics rather than treating the P/F ratio alone.

The relationship matters because Compliance is change in volume divided by change in pressure; elastance is its inverse. Respiratory-system compliance includes both lung and chest-wall contributions. At the bedside, use peak and plateau pressures to separate resistive from elastic loading, then ask whether chest wall, abdomen or lung is responsible.
