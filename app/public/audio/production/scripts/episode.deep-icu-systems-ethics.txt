Now: Why good people still make system errors.

ICU quality, safety & systems. Expert critical care includes preventing iatrogenesis through reliable systems, infection prevention, handoffs, checklists, measurement and quality improvement.

Build the mechanism first. ICU harm often arises from complex-system vulnerabilities: communication failures, device exposure, medication process variation and missed deterioration.

At the bedside, use structured handoffs, daily necessity review, checklists and measured quality cycles while distinguishing process defects from individual blame.

Two traps to actively avoid: Responding to every event with education alone. And: Collecting quality metrics without linking them to a changeable process.

The integration move is this: Expert clinicians improve the system that surrounds individual decisions.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: quality improvement, human factors, handoff.

At the bedside, assess every invasive device for necessity and source potential, obtain appropriate cultures and pair antimicrobial therapy with device/source decisions.

Now: Withholding and withdrawing treatment.

Withholding/withdrawing life support & ICU ethics. Ethically appropriate limitation of life-sustaining treatment depends on patient values, decision authority, proportionality and transparent communication.

Build the mechanism first. Withholding and withdrawing life-sustaining treatment are ethically grounded in patient goals, proportionality and decision authority; stopping a burdensome intervention is not stopping care.

At the bedside, clarify values, prognosis and surrogate authority, recommend a plan aligned with goals and use conflict-resolution resources when disagreement persists.

Two traps to actively avoid: Framing withdrawal as “doing nothing.” And: Offering technically possible treatments without discussing whether they can achieve the patient’s goals.

The integration move is this: Critical-care expertise includes knowing when an intervention no longer serves the person it is meant to help.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: surrogate, proportionality, nonbeneficial treatment.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Separate withdrawal of burdensome treatment from abandonment. Navigate conflict using values, prognosis, institutional support and clear recommendations.

Now: Make a recommendation around patient values.

Goals of care & prognostic communication. High-quality critical care includes eliciting values, communicating uncertainty and aligning burdensome treatments with realistic goals.

Build the mechanism first. Goals-of-care decisions align treatments with the patient’s values and realistic outcomes. Prognosis is probabilistic and changes with response over time.

At the bedside, use best-case/worst-case/most-likely framing and time-limited trials with explicit endpoints when uncertainty remains.

Two traps to actively avoid: Presenting procedures as neutral menu choices without recommendation. And: Using false precision to avoid discussing uncertainty.

The integration move is this: A high-quality critical-care plan includes what outcome the patient would consider worth the burdens of treatment.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: surrogate decision-maker, time-limited trial, best case / worst case.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Explain prognosis without false precision. Frame a time-limited trial with explicit goals and reassessment criteria.

Now: Death determination and prognosis are separate.

Build the mechanism first. Neuroprognostication estimates future neurologic outcome using timed, multimodal evidence. Death by neurologic criteria is a separate formal determination requiring strict prerequisites and examination.

At the bedside, control confounders such as sedatives, hypothermia and metabolic derangement before making high-stakes conclusions.

Two traps to actively avoid: Conflating prognosis with death determination. And: Creating a self-fulfilling prophecy through premature withdrawal based on early uncertain signs.

The integration move is this: Expert communication includes the limits of what is known and the timing required to know more.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: confounder, multimodal prognosis, apnea test.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Separate prognosis from formal death determination. Avoid premature certainty in the presence of sedation, hypothermia, metabolic confounding or insufficient observation time.

Now: Donation after ethical separation.

Organ donation & donor physiology. Organ donation requires separation of death determination from donation decisions and meticulous donor physiology after authorization.

Build the mechanism first. Donation decisions occur only after appropriate death determination pathways and authorization; donor physiology can then require intensive hemodynamic, endocrine and respiratory management.

At the bedside, maintain strict separation between death determination and donation while supporting organs according to established donor-management processes.

Two traps to actively avoid: Allowing donation considerations to influence death determination. And: Assuming critical-care work ends once death by neurologic criteria is established.

The integration move is this: Ethical separation and excellent physiology management are both required.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: donor management, organ procurement organization.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Maintain ethical separation between death determination and donation. Recognize donor hemodynamic/endocrine physiology as active critical care.
