Now: Effusion versus infected pleural space.

Pleural effusion & empyema. Pleural fluid can impair mechanics or represent infection; ultrasound helps characterize volume, complexity and procedural access.

Build the mechanism first. Pleural fluid changes lung/chest-wall mechanics and may reflect hydrostatic, inflammatory, malignant or infected processes; loculation changes drainability.

At the bedside, use ultrasound to assess volume, complexity, diaphragm/lung relationships and a safe procedural window while integrating the cause and need for source control.

Two traps to actively avoid: Draining every effusion simply because it is visible. And: Calling complex septated fluid an uncomplicated transudate based on history alone.

The integration move is this: Pleural ultrasound should answer both “what is it?” and “does it need intervention?”

Keep these terms available in your mental model: septation, empyema, thoracentesis.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Differentiate uncomplicated effusion from infected/complex pleural disease. Use ultrasound and clinical context to guide drainage decisions.
