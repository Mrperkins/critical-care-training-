Abdominal pain after a low-speed crash
Case conference. Abdominal pain after a low-speed crash. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 25-year-old patient is pregnant at 30 weeks and 5 days. After a restrained motor-vehicle collision, the patient develops abdominal pain and frequent contractions. There is no visible vaginal bleeding.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The patient is alert with uterine tenderness and a superficial seat-belt mark. There are no obvious long-bone injuries.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Maternal heart rate is 112 per minute. Maternal blood pressure is 116 over 72. Fetal heart rate is 170 with recurrent decelerations on the referring monitor. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Hemoglobin is 11.2 grams per deciliter. Fibrinogen is 250 milligrams per deciliter and will be interpreted with its trend and clinical context. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. Trauma evaluation includes maternal injuries and pregnancy complications. Abruption remains possible despite normal maternal pressure and no external bleeding. Fetal monitoring contributes information but cannot replace maternal assessment.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Does the apparently minor crash mechanism establish a low-risk pregnancy assessment? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. A modest mechanism does not exclude important placental injury. Pain, contractions and fetal changes require urgent obstetric evaluation alongside trauma care. Priorities. 1. Follow maternal trauma priorities and assess for occult injury. 2. Obtain obstetric consultation and appropriate ongoing fetal monitoring. 3. Trend pain, uterine activity, perfusion and bleeding and prepare for deterioration during transfer.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The patient is transferred to a center capable of trauma and obstetric intervention. The handoff includes crash details, symptoms, monitoring findings and the absence of external bleeding without treating that absence as reassurance. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.

Placental abruption: Trauma-associated abruption
Case synopsis. Placental abruption. Trauma-associated abruption. The pregnancy assessment followed symptoms and monitoring rather than the apparent crash severity. Concealed placental injury remained a time-critical concern. This concludes the case conference. Continue to the disease lesson for the underlying physiology and further study.
