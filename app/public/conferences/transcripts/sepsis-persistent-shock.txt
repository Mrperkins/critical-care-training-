The pressure improves but the patient remains mottled
Case conference. The pressure improves but the patient remains mottled. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 46-year-old patient is being transferred with severe abdominal infection. Antibiotics and initial resuscitation have begun. A vasopressor restores the displayed mean pressure, yet the patient remains poorly perfused with worsening abdominal findings.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The abdomen is distended and tender. Extremities are mottled, urine output remains low, and the team reassesses for persistent shock and an uncontrolled source.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 132 per minute. Mean arterial pressure is 68 on a vasopressor. Respiratory rate is 30 per minute. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Lactate is 6.8, increased from 5.9 millimoles per liter. Arterial pH is 7.25. Creatinine is 2.0 milligrams per deciliter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. The synthetic abdominal CT report describes free air and fluid with suspected bowel perforation. Surgical evaluation and source control are central; pressure restoration alone does not resolve the infection.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Has resuscitation succeeded because mean pressure is now above 65? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. A pressure value is one component of perfusion assessment. Persistent mottling, oliguria and concerning trends require reassessment of circulation, the infection source and complications. Priorities. 1. Escalate urgent surgical source-control planning with the receiving center. 2. Reassess cardiac function, perfusion and fluid tolerance rather than chasing one measurement. 3. Verify antimicrobial and vasoactive delivery and communicate deterioration clearly.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The surgical team prepares definitive intervention while the transport team maintains support. The handoff emphasizes persistent perfusion abnormalities despite improved displayed pressure. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
