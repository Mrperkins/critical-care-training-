Now: Small radius, large resistance consequences.

Pediatric airway & ventilation. Smaller airways, higher metabolic demand and lower reserve make pediatric respiratory deterioration faster and equipment sizing more consequential.

Build the mechanism first. Small airway radius magnifies resistance, metabolic demand is higher and functional residual capacity is smaller, so pediatric respiratory reserve is limited.

At the bedside, anticipate rapid desaturation, apparatus dead-space effects and resistance from small tubes or secretions; size equipment thoughtfully.

Two traps to actively avoid: Using adult ventilator assumptions on small patients. And: Ignoring dead space added by connectors/sensors.

The integration move is this: Tiny geometric changes can create large physiologic consequences.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: airway resistance, dead space, uncuffed/cuffed.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Anticipate rapid desaturation and pressure consequences in small airways. Use age/size-appropriate airway and ventilation strategy.
