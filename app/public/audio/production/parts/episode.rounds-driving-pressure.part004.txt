Now: Driving pressure is not the whole injury model.

Mechanical power. Energy delivered to the respiratory system over time from pressure, volume, flow and respiratory rate.

Build the mechanism first. Mechanical power expresses the energy transferred from the ventilator to the respiratory system per unit time. Pressure, tidal volume, flow and respiratory rate all contribute.

At the bedside, a patient may have an acceptable tidal volume and plateau pressure yet receive a high cumulative energy load because of rate, flow or excessive pressure swings.

Two traps to actively avoid: Treating mechanical power as one magic cutoff. And: Ignoring spontaneous respiratory effort as another source of lung stress.

The integration move is this: Think about injury as repeated energy delivery to vulnerable lung tissue.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: energy load, ventilator-induced lung injury.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Explain how respiratory rate and flow can increase ventilator energy even when tidal volume appears acceptable.
