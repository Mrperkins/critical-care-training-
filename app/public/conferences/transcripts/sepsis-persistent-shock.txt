The pressure improves before the patient does
Case conference. The pressure improves but the patient remains mottled. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 46-year-old patient is being transferred with severe abdominal infection. Antibiotics and initial resuscitation have begun. A vasopressor restores the displayed mean pressure, yet the patient remains poorly perfused with worsening abdominal findings. We arrive after one treatment target has improved. The question is whether that target describes enough of the circulation and whether the underlying infection has been controlled.

Persistent hypoperfusion remains visible
The abdomen is distended and tender. Extremities are mottled, urine output remains low, and the team reassesses for persistent shock and an uncontrolled source. The bedside findings prevent a premature finish line. They tell us the improved pressure has not resolved the whole problem and that the source remains clinically important.

A MAP value is one piece of the response
Heart rate is 132 per minute. Mean arterial pressure is 68 on a vasopressor. Respiratory rate is 30 per minute. The displayed mean pressure is achieved on support. Its meaning depends on what is happening to the rest of the perfusion assessment and the support requirement.

The trajectory is still concerning
Lactate is 6.8, increased from 5.9 millimoles per liter. Arterial pH is 7.25. Creatinine is 2.0 milligrams per deciliter. The lactate has risen, with acidemia and renal dysfunction adding context. No single result explains every mechanism, but the direction agrees with the concern from the bedside.

A source that needs definitive control
The synthetic abdominal CT report describes free air and fluid with suspected bowel perforation. Surgical evaluation and source control are central; pressure restoration alone does not resolve the infection. Support can improve pressure without removing the infectious source. The imaging report changes the definitive plan and the urgency of surgical coordination.

Continue support while pursuing source control
A pressure value is one component of perfusion assessment. Persistent mottling, oliguria and concerning trends require reassessment of circulation, the infection source and complications. Escalate urgent surgical source-control planning with the receiving center. Reassess cardiac function, perfusion and fluid tolerance rather than chasing one measurement. Verify antimicrobial and vasoactive delivery and communicate deterioration clearly. Treatment delivery also deserves a check. An escalating patient needs confirmation that the intended support is reaching them, alongside assessment of what the support cannot fix on its own.

Handoff the unresolved perfusion problem
The surgical team prepares definitive intervention while the transport team maintains support. The handoff emphasizes persistent perfusion abnormalities despite improved displayed pressure. Say explicitly that pressure improved while mottling and oliguria persisted. That statement gives the next team the reason to continue reassessment despite the apparently acceptable number.
