Now: Protein, mobility and recovery.

ICU-acquired weakness & recovery. Critical illness, immobility, inflammation, sedation and organ failure can produce profound weakness and long-term functional loss.

Build the mechanism first. Inflammation, immobility, neuropathy, myopathy, sedation and undernutrition can cause profound ICU-acquired weakness and prolonged post-ICU disability.

At the bedside, build mobility, delirium prevention, nutrition and sedation minimization into daily care rather than starting “rehab” only after organ failure resolves.

Two traps to actively avoid: Interpreting generalized weakness only as deconditioning. And: Separating recovery planning from ventilator liberation.

The integration move is this: Survival is not the only outcome; function is a critical-care endpoint.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: critical illness myopathy, critical illness neuropathy, PICS.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Recognize weakness as an ICU outcome, not merely a discharge problem. Connect sedation, mobility, nutrition and ventilator liberation to recovery.
