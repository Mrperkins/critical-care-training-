The quieter child with stridor
Case conference. The quieter child with stridor. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 3-year-old child, measured weight 14 kilograms, has worsening noisy breathing. The initial barking cough is followed by increasing distress. During assessment the child becomes drowsy and the stridor becomes softer.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. There is severe recession, poor air movement and reduced interaction. The team considers croup alongside bacterial tracheitis, epiglottitis and foreign-body obstruction.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 168 per minute. Respiratory rate has fallen to 22 with reduced effort. Oxygen saturation is 86 percent on supplemental oxygen. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Laboratory sampling is deferred because it must not delay airway support. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. A critically narrowed airway must be supported before diagnostic imaging. Avoid upsetting the child with unnecessary throat examination or positioning. Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Reduced noise may reflect reduced airflow and exhaustion. An atypical or poorly responsive course broadens the differential and calls for expert airway support. Priorities. 1. Summon pediatric airway expertise immediately and prepare a coordinated rescue plan. 2. Support oxygenation and ventilation while minimizing agitation. 3. Treat the likely cause under protocol without sending an unstable child away for imaging.
