A seizure five days after delivery
Case conference. A seizure five days after delivery. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 32-year-old patient is five days postpartum after an apparently uncomplicated delivery. A severe headache and visual disturbance are followed by a generalized seizure. The family did not realize a pregnancy-related emergency could occur after discharge.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. After the seizure the patient is confused, with spontaneous breathing and no obvious trauma. The team evaluates glucose and considers intracranial hemorrhage, thrombosis and other seizure causes alongside eclampsia.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 118 per minute. Blood pressure is 182 over 116. Respiratory rate is 24 per minute. Saturation is 93 percent on room air. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Glucose is 106 milligrams per deciliter. Platelet count is 156 billion per liter. Creatinine is 1.0 milligrams per deciliter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. Postpartum timing does not exclude eclampsia. Atypical features, focal deficits or persistent altered mental status increase the need for urgent neurological evaluation and imaging after stabilization.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Does a completed delivery remove eclampsia from the differential? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Eclampsia can occur postpartum and without prior recognition of preeclampsia. Simultaneous evaluation of alternative seizure causes prevents premature closure. Priorities. 1. Protect airway and ventilation, prevent injury and treat suspected eclampsia with magnesium under protocol. 2. Treat persistent severe hypertension and arrange immediate obstetric consultation. 3. Reassess neurological recovery and pursue urgent imaging or additional diagnostics when indicated.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The seizure stops and the team continues close respiratory and neurological monitoring. The handoff explicitly states postpartum day five, because pregnancy history remains relevant after delivery. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
