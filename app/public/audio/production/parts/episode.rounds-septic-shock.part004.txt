Now connect Septic shock physiology to Source control. Definitive management of infected material, devitalized tissue or an infected device can be as important as antimicrobial therapy.

The relationship matters because Antibiotics cannot sterilize every infected collection, necrotic tissue or infected device. Persistent nidus can sustain inflammatory and microbial burden despite apparently appropriate antimicrobial therapy. At the bedside, ask early whether drainage, debridement, device removal or surgery is required and whether it is actually happening.

Now connect Septic shock physiology to Lactate physiology. Lactate reflects production and clearance across multiple pathways and is not a direct tissue-hypoxia meter.

The relationship matters because Lactate rises from multiple pathways including adrenergic glycolysis, impaired clearance and cellular metabolic changes; it is not a direct meter of tissue hypoxia. At the bedside, trend lactate with perfusion, liver function, catecholamine exposure and clinical trajectory. A falling value can be reassuring but should not override a deteriorating patient.

Now: Macrocirculation and microcirculation can disagree.

Oxygen extraction. The relationship between delivered and consumed oxygen reflected by venous saturation and tissue extraction.

Build the mechanism first. Tissues can increase extraction when delivery falls, lowering venous oxygen saturation until extraction reserve is exhausted. High venous saturation can also be abnormal when extraction is impaired or flow is maldistributed.

At the bedside, interpret SvO2 or ScvO2 alongside cardiac output, hemoglobin, arterial saturation, metabolic demand and shock phenotype.

Two traps to actively avoid: Calling a high venous saturation reassuring in every patient. And: Treating low venous saturation as a diagnosis instead of a physiologic clue.

The integration move is this: Use venous saturation to ask whether delivery and demand are appropriately matched.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: O2ER, SvO2, critical DO2.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Interpret low or high venous saturation in context rather than labeling it simply good or bad.

Now connect Oxygen extraction to Shock phenotyping. Identify inadequate effective circulation by integrating flow, vascular tone, congestion, oxygen delivery and organ response.

The relationship matters because Shock is inadequate effective tissue perfusion and oxygen utilization, not merely hypotension. Flow, vascular tone, capacitance, congestion and extraction can fail in different combinations. At the bedside, phenotype the circulation repeatedly. A patient can move from one dominant mechanism to another during resuscitation.
