Now: Two life threats, competing priorities.

Severe traumatic brain injury. TBI management centers on preventing secondary brain injury by protecting oxygenation, perfusion and intracranial dynamics.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: secondary injury, CPP, intracranial compliance.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Prioritize avoidance of hypoxemia and hypotension. Integrate ICP, CPP, ventilation and extracranial injuries without creating competing harm.

Massive transfusion & hemorrhagic resuscitation. Hemorrhagic resuscitation integrates source control, balanced component replacement, calcium, temperature, coagulation and perfusion.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: M T P, fibrinogen, ionized calcium, lethal triad.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Run the resuscitation as a system rather than as isolated blood-unit administration. Recognize dilutional/coagulopathic, hypocalcemic and hypothermic deterioration early.

Now: CPP meets hemorrhage.

ICP, CPP & cerebral perfusion. Cerebral perfusion depends on the gradient across the cerebral circulation and is influenced by pressure, venous outflow, CO2 and intracranial compliance.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: CPP, autoregulation, Monro-Kellie.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Predict how systemic pressure, CO2 and venous pressure affect cerebral perfusion. Recognize when a normal MAP does not guarantee adequate CPP.

Multisystem treatment tradeoffs. A therapy that helps one organ can worsen another; expert care weighs global benefit, reversibility and monitoring.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Explain at least two competing organ effects of a proposed intervention. Choose the least harmful testable strategy when the optimal answer is uncertain.

Tie this back to the patient. Ask what variable is changing, what downstream effect you expect, and what bedside finding would support or contradict that model.

Now: Coagulopathy and brain injury.

Critical-illness coagulopathy. Bleeding and thrombosis reflect platelets, fibrinogen, coagulation factors, fibrinolysis, temperature, calcium, dilution and disease-specific pathways.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: fibrinolysis, fibrinogen, viscoelastic testing.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Reason from the failing component of clot formation rather than treating INR alone. Anticipate temperature, calcium and dilution effects during hemorrhagic resuscitation.

Tie this back to the patient. Ask what variable is changing, what downstream effect you expect, and what bedside finding would support or contradict that model.

Clinical uncertainty & model revision. Expert reasoning explicitly tracks uncertainty, contradictory data and what finding would falsify the current working model.
