The handoff says weakness
Case conference. Confusion without fever. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 79-year-old patient has dysuria followed by new confusion. The family reports reduced intake and urine output. The patient is cold rather than febrile. The initial handoff describes “weakness,” but the examination suggests a systemic illness. The word weakness compresses a complex story into a low-urgency label. Reconstruct the change from baseline, especially the new confusion and reduced urine output.

The examination suggests systemic illness
There is altered attention, mottling and delayed refill. The team evaluates infection and other causes of shock and delirium. These are findings about perfusion and organ function. They deserve an urgent explanation even when the temperature does not follow the familiar fever pattern.

Hypothermia does not remove infection from consideration
Heart rate is 114 per minute. Blood pressure is 82 over 46. Respiratory rate is 28 per minute. Temperature is 35.3 degrees Celsius. The low temperature sits beside tachycardia, tachypnea and hypotension. Read that cluster before deciding that an afebrile patient is unlikely to have a serious infection.

Organ dysfunction and a possible source
Lactate is 5.1 millimoles per liter. Creatinine is 2.2 milligrams per deciliter, compared with a baseline of 0.9. Urinalysis shows pyuria, interpreted with symptoms and the broader assessment. The creatinine change adds a comparison with baseline. The urine findings fit part of the history, but they do not prove that every feature comes from a urinary source.

Treat suspected sepsis; continue the source assessment
Suspected infection, organ dysfunction and poor perfusion prompt immediate sepsis management. The team avoids declaring a definitive urinary source from urinalysis alone. A working diagnosis can support timely treatment without becoming a fixed conclusion. The team still needs to evaluate source control needs and alternatives to septic shock.

Act on illness severity while clarifying the source
Older adults may present with confusion or hypothermia. The source remains a working diagnosis while resuscitation and evaluation proceed. Deliver timely antimicrobials and obtain appropriate cultures without avoidable treatment delay. Resuscitate and assess fluid responsiveness and tolerance; begin indicated vasoactive support under protocol. Evaluate source control needs and alternative causes of shock. The first interventions should produce information as well as support. Reassess the circulation and the suspected source instead of letting the initial label replace the next examination.

Revisit pressure, perfusion and urine output
After initial treatment, the team repeats perfusion assessment and reviews the pressure and urine trend. An improving number does not remove the need for source evaluation. A better monitor number can occur before the patient’s organ function has recovered. The handoff should make clear which parts of the response are established and which remain unresolved.

Sepsis in an older adult: the presentation may be cold and confused
