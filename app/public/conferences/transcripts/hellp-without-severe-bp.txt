The laboratory results are worse than the pressure
Case conference. The laboratory results are worse than the pressure. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 26-year-old patient is 18 hours postpartum. Persistent upper abdominal pain, nausea and profound malaise continue after delivery. The blood pressure has not reached the severe range, and the initial concern is postoperative discomfort.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The patient is pale with right upper quadrant tenderness and new petechiae around venipuncture sites. Mental status and breathing are currently normal.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 108 per minute. Blood pressure is 142 over 90. Saturation is 98 percent. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Platelet count is 48 billion per liter. A S T is 340 units per liter. L D H is 1080 units per liter. Haptoglobin is low, supporting hemolysis in this context. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. HELLP can present postpartum and without severe hypertension. Other thrombotic microangiopathies and liver disorders must also be considered by the specialist team.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Can a blood pressure of 142 over 90 exclude a serious pregnancy-related microangiopathy? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. The absence of severe hypertension does not negate major platelet, liver and hemolysis abnormalities. Delivery also does not guarantee immediate resolution. Priorities. 1. Obtain urgent obstetric and critical care review and trend the laboratory abnormalities. 2. Assess for active bleeding and evolving hepatic or neurological complications. 3. Plan monitored transfer and specialist management rather than waiting for severe hypertension to develop.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The platelet count is repeated and the clinical course is monitored closely. The handoff emphasizes the postpartum timing and the trajectory of symptoms and laboratories. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
