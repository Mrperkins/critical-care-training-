Suspected abruption is a clinical emergency
The clinical pattern raises urgent concern for abruption. Blood can be concealed behind the placenta. A reassuring ultrasound does not reliably exclude the diagnosis.  A scan that fails to show the cause does not make this examination disappear. The team has to act on the clinical pattern and the risk of delay.

Resuscitate and coordinate definitive obstetric care
External bleeding can underestimate total hemorrhage. Maternal resuscitation and urgent obstetric action are needed when pain, uterine findings and fetal compromise suggest abruption. Activate urgent obstetric and hemorrhage support and coordinate the fastest appropriate definitive care. Resuscitate the mother with positioning, access and indicated blood products under protocol. Continue maternal and fetal assessment without delaying action for a scan. The destination and timing are part of the treatment. Support during transfer must be coordinated with a team able to provide the definitive obstetric intervention the patient may require.
