A barking cough in the night
Case conference. A barking cough in the night. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 2-year-old child, measured weight 12 kilograms, has a viral prodrome. The child wakes with a barking cough and noisy inspiration. Stridor persists while calm on a caregiver’s lap.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The child is alert, with inspiratory stridor and moderate sternal recession. There is no drooling, tripod posture or reported choking. The voice is hoarse.

The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 142 per minute. Respiratory rate is 34 per minute. Oxygen saturation is 96 percent on room air. Temperature is 38.1 degrees Celsius. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Upper-airway narrowing may be important before oxygen saturation falls. Stridor at rest, recession, behavior and air entry guide severity assessment. Priorities. 1. Keep the child calm and minimize distressing interventions. 2. Use corticosteroid and nebulized epinephrine when indicated by the clinical pathway. 3. Observe for recurrence and escalate if response is inadequate or the presentation is atypical.
