Now: What the balloon can and cannot do.

Intra-aortic balloon pump physiology. I A B P timing uses diastolic augmentation and presystolic deflation to alter coronary perfusion and LV afterload.

Build the mechanism first. I A B P inflates in diastole to augment aortic diastolic pressure and deflates before systole to reduce LV afterload. Benefit depends on timing and native rhythm/hemodynamics.

At the bedside, use the arterial waveform to judge inflation near the dicrotic notch and presystolic deflation; compare assisted and unassisted beats.

Two traps to actively avoid: Treating augmentation number alone as success. And: Allowing late deflation to increase LV afterload.

The integration move is this: The waveform should show you the mechanism the device is trying to create.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: diastolic augmentation, deflation, trigger.

Now: Timing errors on the waveform.

Arterial line. Placement, setup, leveling, zeroing, waveform interpretation and troubleshooting of invasive arterial pressure monitoring.

Build the mechanism first. An arterial catheter transmits pressure through a fluid-filled system to a transducer. Accuracy depends on leveling, zeroing and the dynamic response of the tubing-transducer system.

At the bedside, read the waveform before trusting systolic and diastolic values. Mean pressure is often less distorted by damping than pulse extremes.

Two traps to actively avoid: Treating a monitor number as direct truth. And: Ignoring hydrostatic error from transducer height.

The integration move is this: The patient, catheter, tubing, transducer and monitor form one measurement system.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: phlebostatic axis, dynamic response.

The relationship matters because Overdamping blunts high-frequency components and narrows pulse pressure; underdamping/resonance exaggerates oscillations and can overstate systolic pressure.
