Now: What alveolar dead space does to the gap.

Dead space. Ventilation that does not effectively participate in CO2 exchange, from conducting airways or ventilated but poorly perfused alveoli.

Build the mechanism first. Dead-space ventilation reaches regions that do not effectively eliminate CO2 because perfusion is absent or inadequate, or because gas remains in conducting airways.

At the bedside, rising minute-ventilation requirement, widening PaCO2–EtCO2 gap or unexpectedly low EtCO2 can signal increasing dead space when ventilation is otherwise unchanged.

Two traps to actively avoid: Equating all hypercapnia with low minute ventilation. And: Using EtCO2 as a fixed conversion to PaCO2 in shock.

The integration move is this: CO2 is a ventilation-perfusion signal as well as a ventilation signal.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: VD/VT, alveolar dead space.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Use PaCO2–EtCO2 behavior and minute ventilation requirements to reason about changing dead space.

Reasoning checkpoint. Do not just repeat the statement: Use PaCO2–EtCO2 behavior and minute ventilation requirements to reason about changing dead space. Talk yourself through what data would support that conclusion, what data would contradict it, and what intervention could test the model without committing the patient to unnecessary risk.

Now connect Dead space to Pulmonary embolism physiology. Acute pulmonary vascular obstruction can abruptly raise RV afterload, impair LV filling and cause shock despite structurally normal lungs.

The relationship matters because Large pulmonary embolic obstruction abruptly raises RV afterload, causing RV dilation, reduced LV filling and potential obstructive shock; gas exchange often reflects increased dead space. At the bedside, look for RV strain, low-flow signs and widening PaCO2–EtCO2 gap; be cautious with induction and excessive positive pressure in a preload- and RV-dependent circulation.

Now connect Dead space to A R D S physiology. Diffuse inflammatory lung injury producing heterogeneity, shunt, low aerated lung volume and risk from ventilator-induced injury.

The relationship matters because A R D S produces heterogeneous inflammatory lung injury, shunt, edema and a reduced aerated “baby lung,” making regional overdistension possible even at seemingly modest tidal volumes. At the bedside, integrate gas exchange, compliance, recruitability, prone response, driving pressure and hemodynamics rather than treating the P/F ratio alone.

Shock phenotyping. Identify inadequate effective circulation by integrating flow, vascular tone, congestion, oxygen delivery and organ response.

Build the mechanism first. Shock is inadequate effective tissue perfusion and oxygen utilization, not merely hypotension. Flow, vascular tone, capacitance, congestion and extraction can fail in different combinations.
