Now connect Preeclampsia & eclampsia to ICP, CPP & cerebral perfusion. Cerebral perfusion depends on the gradient across the cerebral circulation and is influenced by pressure, venous outflow, CO2 and intracranial compliance.

The relationship matters because CPP is the pressure gradient available to drive cerebral blood flow and is influenced by arterial pressure, ICP, venous pressure and autoregulation. Intracranial compliance falls as compensatory reserve is exhausted. At the bedside, interpret ICP as a dynamic system with waveform, position, venous drainage, CO2 and sedation effects. A “normal” MAP does not guarantee adequate cerebral perfusion.

Now connect Preeclampsia & eclampsia to Critical-illness coagulopathy. Bleeding and thrombosis reflect platelets, fibrinogen, coagulation factors, fibrinolysis, temperature, calcium, dilution and disease-specific pathways.

The relationship matters because Hemostasis depends on platelets, fibrinogen, coagulation factors, fibrinolysis, calcium, pH and temperature. Critical illness can produce simultaneous bleeding and thrombosis risk. At the bedside, interpret conventional labs and viscoelastic tests in the clinical context and replace the failing component rather than one arbitrary number.

Now: Stabilization and definitive obstetric management.

Preeclampsia & eclampsia. Hypertensive disease of pregnancy can involve cerebral, hepatic, renal, hematologic and placental dysfunction with seizure and pulmonary-edema risk.

Build the mechanism first. Preeclampsia is a multisystem endothelial/placental disorder that can cause severe hypertension, cerebral edema/seizure, renal injury, pulmonary edema, liver injury and thrombocytopenia.

At the bedside, recognize severe features across organ systems, prevent/treat seizure and control dangerous pressure while coordinating definitive obstetric management.

Two traps to actively avoid: Focusing only on blood pressure. And: Giving large fluid loads to a patient with endothelial leak and pulmonary-edema risk.

The integration move is this: Maternal stabilization and obstetric management are linked, not sequential.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: severe features, magnesium, HELLP.
