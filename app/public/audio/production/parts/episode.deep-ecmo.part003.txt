Now connect A R D S physiology to Driving pressure. Plateau pressure minus total peep; a bedside expression of tidal strain relative to respiratory-system compliance.

The relationship matters because Driving pressure is plateau pressure minus total peep and reflects tidal pressure applied to the respiratory system relative to available compliance. At the bedside, a fixed tidal volume can impose very different stress depending on how much functional lung is available. Trend driving pressure alongside plateau pressure and mechanics.

Now connect A R D S physiology to Pulmonary vascular resistance. Resistance faced by the RV, influenced by lung volume, oxygenation, CO2, pH, pulmonary vascular disease and mechanical ventilation.

The relationship matters because Pulmonary vascular resistance reflects the combined behavior of alveolar and extra-alveolar vessels and is affected by lung volume, oxygen, CO2, pH and pulmonary vascular disease. At the bedside, both severe derecruitment and overdistension can raise RV afterload. Hypoxemia, hypercapnia and acidosis can compound the problem.

Now: VA: flow changes loading conditions.

E C M O circuit & physiology literacy. Extracorporeal support changes preload, afterload, gas exchange and oxygen delivery through a circuit whose configuration determines what it can support.

Build the mechanism first. E C M O drains venous blood, pumps it through a membrane lung and returns it to the circulation. VV supports gas exchange; VA additionally supports systemic flow and changes cardiac loading conditions.

At the bedside, follow blood through drainage, pump, oxygenator and return while considering native heart/lung function and cannula position.

Two traps to actively avoid: Equating pump RPM with effective support. And: Ignoring recirculation, LV distension or differential hypoxemia.

The integration move is this: The circuit and the native circulation are one coupled system.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: VV E C M O, VA E C M O, sweep gas, recirculation, differential hypoxemia.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Distinguish VV from VA support goals and limitations. Reason from circuit flow, native heart/lung function and cannula position to common failure patterns.
