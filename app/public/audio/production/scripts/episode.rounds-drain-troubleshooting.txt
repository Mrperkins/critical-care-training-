This is a level 3 critical-care session. The goal is not to memorize a list. The goal is to build a model that lets you predict what the patient will do next.

By the end, you should be able to troubleshoot pleural drainage from patient outward; and use system behavior and pleural ultrasound to distinguish persistent pathology from device failure.

Now: Start with the patient.

Pleural drain troubleshooting. A pleural drain must be assessed as patient + tube + connections + drainage system rather than as an isolated device.

Build the mechanism first. A pleural drain can fail because pathology persists, the tube is malpositioned/occluded, connections leak, or the drainage system is configured incorrectly.

At the bedside, trace from patient to skin entry, tubing, connections, water seal and suction; correlate with respiratory/hemodynamic changes and pleural imaging.

Two traps to actively avoid: Milking or manipulating tubing without understanding the problem. And: Assuming bubbling always means the same thing.

The integration move is this: Device behavior is meaningful only when interpreted with the patient and the physical circuit.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: water seal, tidaling, air leak.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Trace a failing drain from the patient outward. Differentiate expected system behavior from kink, disconnection, migration, occlusion and ongoing pleural leak.

Now: Use pleural ultrasound when the physiology and system disagree.

Pneumothorax ultrasound. Pleural ultrasound combines sliding, B-lines, M-mode patterns and lung point rather than relying on a single sign.

Build the mechanism first. Pleural ultrasound infers apposition or separation of pleural layers through sliding, B-lines, lung pulse, M-mode patterns and lung point.

At the bedside, absent sliding raises suspicion but is nonspecific; lung point is more specific when present in the appropriate context.

Two traps to actively avoid: Diagnosing pneumothorax from absent sliding alone. And: Forgetting apnea, pleurodesis, mainstem intubation or severe lung disease as alternatives.

The integration move is this: Use multiple signs and the patient’s physiology rather than one binary image feature.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: lung sliding, lung point, seashore, barcode.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Use multiple pleural signs to support or weaken the diagnosis. Recognize situations where absent sliding is not pneumothorax.
