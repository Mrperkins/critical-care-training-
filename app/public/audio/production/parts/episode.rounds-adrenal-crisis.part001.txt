Now: The physiology behind the clues.

Adrenal crisis. Cortisol deficiency can cause refractory hypotension, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypoglycemia and shock under stress.

Build the mechanism first. Cortisol deficiency impairs vascular responsiveness and stress metabolism; mineralocorticoid deficiency can add sodium loss and hyperkalemia depending on the cause.

At the bedside, consider it in refractory shock with compatible history or electrolyte/glucose clues and recognize that treatment can rapidly change vasopressor needs.

Two traps to actively avoid: Waiting for confirmatory testing in an unstable high-suspicion patient. And: Assuming normal potassium excludes all adrenal insufficiency.

The integration move is this: Endocrine failure can masquerade as undifferentiated shock.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: cortisol, mineralocorticoid, stress dose.

Now: Why the circulation stops responding normally.

Shock phenotyping. Identify inadequate effective circulation by integrating flow, vascular tone, congestion, oxygen delivery and organ response.

Build the mechanism first. Shock is inadequate effective tissue perfusion and oxygen utilization, not merely hypotension. Flow, vascular tone, capacitance, congestion and extraction can fail in different combinations.

At the bedside, phenotype the circulation repeatedly. A patient can move from one dominant mechanism to another during resuscitation.

Two traps to actively avoid: Naming shock once and never revisiting the model. And: Treating MAP as the only endpoint.
