Burn shock & resuscitation. Major burns create capillary leak, evaporative loss, inflammatory shock and evolving fluid requirements that cannot be managed by formula alone.

Build the mechanism first. Major burns create inflammatory capillary leak, evaporative loss and changing vascular permeability. Formula estimates cannot account for individual response or fluid creep.

At the bedside, use a formula as an initial estimate, then titrate to perfusion and organ endpoints while watching for over-resuscitation.

Two traps to actively avoid: Treating the formula as a fixed prescription. And: Chasing urine output with unlimited crystalloid despite worsening edema/compartment physiology.

The integration move is this: Burn resuscitation is dynamic shock management, not arithmetic.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: TBSA, burn shock, urine output.

Now connect Burn shock & resuscitation to Inhalation injury. Thermal upper-airway injury, chemical tracheobronchial injury and systemic toxic exposure are distinct problems that can coexist after fire.

The relationship matters because Fire exposure can cause thermal upper-airway edema, chemical lower-airway injury and systemic carbon monoxide/cyanide toxicity—three distinct processes. At the bedside, assess for progressive airway edema, bronchial injury and systemic toxin physiology separately; early airway control may be safer before edema peaks in selected patients.

The relationship matters because Hemostasis depends on platelets, fibrinogen, coagulation factors, fibrinolysis, calcium, pH and temperature. Critical illness can produce simultaneous bleeding and thrombosis risk. At the bedside, interpret conventional labs and viscoelastic tests in the clinical context and replace the failing component rather than one arbitrary number.
