The uterus firms, but bleeding continues
Case conference. Heavy bleeding with a firm uterus. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 34-year-old patient is 45 minutes postpartum after an instrument-assisted birth. The patient has ongoing bleeding despite uterine measures. The uterus is now firm, and the estimated loss exceeds 1,500 milliliters. The team has treated a plausible cause, and one examination finding has improved. The unresolved question is why the patient is still bleeding.

The source needs another look
The patient is pale and anxious. The referring team reports concern for a genital tract laceration and an incomplete placental examination. The firm uterus is useful new information. It should shift attention toward other sources instead of being interpreted as proof that the overall hemorrhage is controlled.

The circulation is still under threat
Heart rate is 140 per minute. Blood pressure is 82 over 46. Temperature is 35.8 degrees Celsius. The pressure and heart rate show why the reassessment must be urgent. The low temperature adds another problem to manage during ongoing hemorrhage resuscitation.

Bleeding and coagulation must be followed together
Hemoglobin is 8.1 grams per deciliter. Fibrinogen is 145 milligrams per deciliter. Platelet count is 94 billion per liter. The laboratory results describe part of the evolving consequence of blood loss. They reinforce the need for coordinated resuscitation while the team finds and controls the source.

Tone is only one part of the source assessment
A firm uterus with persistent bleeding prompts urgent evaluation for trauma, retained tissue and coagulopathy. Successful treatment of one cause does not exclude another. A treatment response can teach us what remains unexplained. Here improved tone does not account for the continued loss, so the next assessment has to broaden.

Continue resuscitation; change the source strategy
Repeating the same atony intervention without reassessing the source can delay definitive control. Multiple causes of postpartum hemorrhage may coexist. Continue hemorrhage resuscitation and obtain immediate obstetric evaluation for source control. Communicate the firm uterus, delivery instrumentation and uncertainty about placental completeness. Maintain warming, blood product coordination and serial coagulation assessment under the hemorrhage pathway. The resuscitation continues while the source plan changes. Make the reason for the change explicit so the team stays coordinated through repair or other definitive management.

Handoff the sequence, not one total
The obstetric team proceeds to definitive assessment and repair while resuscitation continues. The transfer timeline includes all interventions and ongoing bleeding rather than a single estimated total. A single estimate hides whether the patient lost blood before or after an intervention. The timeline explains what worked, what did not and how much uncertainty remains.
