Choose care that can address both emergencies
A modest mechanism does not exclude important placental injury. Pain, contractions and fetal changes require urgent obstetric evaluation alongside trauma care. Follow maternal trauma priorities and assess for occult injury. Obtain obstetric consultation and appropriate ongoing fetal monitoring. Trend pain, uterine activity, perfusion and bleeding and prepare for deterioration during transfer. The plan should remain useful if the patient worsens en route. That means sharing the current symptoms and monitoring changes, with a destination able to respond to both maternal injury and obstetric complications.

Communicate symptoms and trajectory
The patient is transferred to a center capable of trauma and obstetric intervention. The handoff includes crash details, symptoms, monitoring findings and the absence of external bleeding without treating that absence as reassurance. A good handoff does not reduce this to a minor collision. It preserves the evidence that changed the risk assessment after the mechanism was known.
