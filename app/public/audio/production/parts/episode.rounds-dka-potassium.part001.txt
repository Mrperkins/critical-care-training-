Build the mechanism first. Insulin deficiency and counter-regulatory hormones drive ketogenesis, osmotic diuresis and major total-body electrolyte deficits even when serum levels initially appear normal or high.

At the bedside, track volume status, potassium, sodium/osmolality, anion gap and acid-base trajectory through treatment.

Two traps to actively avoid: Treating glucose normalization as resolution of DKA. And: Forgetting that insulin can rapidly expose profound potassium depletion.
