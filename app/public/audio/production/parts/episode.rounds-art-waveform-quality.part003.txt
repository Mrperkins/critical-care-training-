Now: Use the dynamic response test before changing therapy.

Arterial waveform damping & resonance. The monitoring system can distort systolic and diastolic pressures while mean pressure is relatively preserved.

Build the mechanism first. Overdamping blunts high-frequency components and narrows pulse pressure; underdamping/resonance exaggerates oscillations and can overstate systolic pressure.

At the bedside, use a fast-flush or dynamic-response assessment and inspect for air, clot, tubing compliance, extra stopcocks and catheter position.

Two traps to actively avoid: Changing vasoactive therapy before checking waveform quality. And: Assuming all waveform distortion comes from the catheter tip.

The integration move is this: Validate the measurement system before treating the measurement.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: square-wave test, natural frequency.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Recognize over- and under-damped systems and troubleshoot tubing, bubbles, clot and excessive resonance.
