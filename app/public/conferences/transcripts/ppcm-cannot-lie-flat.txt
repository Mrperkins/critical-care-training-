“I cannot breathe lying down”
Case conference. “I cannot breathe lying down”. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 33-year-old patient is three weeks postpartum with no known cardiac disease. Progressive dyspnea and leg swelling have been attributed to recovery and sleep loss. The patient now sleeps sitting upright and has a cough.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. There are bilateral crackles, elevated neck veins and peripheral edema. The patient is tachypneic but alert.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 122 per minute. Blood pressure is 118 over 76. Respiratory rate is 30 per minute. Saturation is 89 percent on room air. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. B N P is 1,480 picograms per milliliter. Creatinine is 1.1 milligrams per deciliter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. A synthetic echocardiography report describes global left-ventricular systolic dysfunction with ejection fraction 28 percent. Chest imaging shows pulmonary congestion. Peripartum cardiomyopathy is considered after evaluation for other causes; BNP alone does not establish the diagnosis. In visual mode, an optional real reference image can be opened. It belongs to a different patient and is not the scan from this fictional case.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Which findings distinguish this from expected postpartum fatigue or isolated dependent edema? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Orthopnea, hypoxemia, pulmonary findings and systolic dysfunction indicate pathological heart failure. Pulmonary embolism and other causes of postpartum dyspnea must also be evaluated. Priorities. 1. Support oxygenation and consider noninvasive respiratory support when appropriate. 2. Coordinate cardiology and obstetric care and treat congestion under a pregnancy-aware heart-failure pathway. 3. Monitor perfusion and rhythm and avoid reflexive large fluid administration.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. With respiratory and congestion-directed treatment, breathing improves. Cardiac follow-up and a longer-term plan are arranged; early symptom relief does not establish recovery of ventricular function. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
