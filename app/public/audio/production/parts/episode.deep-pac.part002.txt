Now: Bad measurement creates bad physiology.

Pulmonary artery catheter interpretation. A PA catheter is useful only when waveforms, zero/level, thermodilution assumptions and the clinical model are integrated correctly.

Build the mechanism first. A pulmonary artery catheter samples pressures through right heart and pulmonary circulation and can estimate flow and oxygen extraction. Every derived variable depends on valid waveforms and measurement assumptions.

At the bedside, level and zero first, recognize RA/RV/PA/wedge waveforms, validate wedge and interpret serial pressures with cardiac output and SvO2.

Two traps to actively avoid: Treating a wedge number without validating the waveform. And: Using one hemodynamic snapshot as a diagnosis.

The integration move is this: Use invasive hemodynamics to test and refine a model, not to collect isolated targets.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: RAP, PAP, PAOP, thermodilution, P V R, S V R.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Recognize chamber waveforms during insertion and interpret pressures in physiologic context. Use serial values to test a hemodynamic model rather than treating targets in isolation.

Now connect Pulmonary artery catheter interpretation to Oxygen extraction. The relationship between delivered and consumed oxygen reflected by venous saturation and tissue extraction.

The relationship matters because Tissues can increase extraction when delivery falls, lowering venous oxygen saturation until extraction reserve is exhausted. High venous saturation can also be abnormal when extraction is impaired or flow is maldistributed. At the bedside, interpret SvO2 or ScvO2 alongside cardiac output, hemoglobin, arterial saturation, metabolic demand and shock phenotype.

Now connect Pulmonary artery catheter interpretation to Shock phenotyping. Identify inadequate effective circulation by integrating flow, vascular tone, congestion, oxygen delivery and organ response.

The relationship matters because Shock is inadequate effective tissue perfusion and oxygen utilization, not merely hypotension. Flow, vascular tone, capacitance, congestion and extraction can fail in different combinations. At the bedside, phenotype the circulation repeatedly. A patient can move from one dominant mechanism to another during resuscitation.
