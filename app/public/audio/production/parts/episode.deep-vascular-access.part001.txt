Ultrasound-guided peripheral IV. Ultrasound-guided peripheral access combines vessel selection, needle-tip tracking, catheter length and post-placement assessment.

Build the mechanism first. Ultrasound-guided peripheral access depends on selecting a vessel whose depth, diameter and course match the catheter and on tracking the actual needle tip.

At the bedside, scan before puncture, use multiple planes, advance enough catheter into the lumen and confirm function without soft-tissue infiltration.

Two traps to actively avoid: Following the needle shaft while losing the tip. And: Using a catheter too short for a deep vessel.

The integration move is this: Success is durable intraluminal catheter position, not merely blood return.

Intraosseous access. Rapid vascular access through the medullary space when conventional access is delayed or unreliable.

Build the mechanism first. Intraosseous access reaches the medullary venous network through bone cortex, providing rapid vascular access when conventional access is delayed.

At the bedside, use the approved landmark and device-specific technique, then confirm stability, flush behavior and surrounding tissue integrity repeatedly.

Two traps to actively avoid: Assuming every device that enters bone is intramedullary and usable. And: Forgetting ongoing extravasation/compartment-risk checks.

The integration move is this: Speed matters, but landmark discipline and confirmation remain part of the procedure.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: cortex, medullary space, extravasation.
