Reperfusion with a circulation-aware plan
A time-critical ECG pattern must be acted on in its clinical context. Right-ventricular involvement and hypotension require careful attention to preload, rhythm and medication effects. Activate the appropriate acute coronary occlusion and reperfusion pathway. Avoid treatments that worsen existing hypotension; reassess perfusion and volume responsiveness individually. Monitor for bradyarrhythmia and conduction block and prepare indicated support. Urgent coronary care and bedside stabilization proceed together. The receiving team needs to know why pressure, preload and rhythm are part of this particular infarction handoff.

Keep the ECG and perfusion timeline visible
The receiving catheterization team is notified of the ECG pattern, right-sided findings and unstable pressure. Serial ECGs and perfusion checks continue during stabilization. The handoff should say what was present before treatment as well as after it. That distinction helps the next team interpret any new pressure or rhythm change.
