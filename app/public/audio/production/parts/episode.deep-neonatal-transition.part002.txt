The relationship matters because Positive intrathoracic pressure can reduce the gradient for venous return, change transmural ventricular pressures and alter P V R depending on lung volume and recruitability. Now: Ductus and foramen ovale change roles.

Neonatal cardiopulmonary transition. Birth requires a rapid shift from placental to pulmonary gas exchange with falling P V R and changing shunt flow through the ductus and foramen ovale.

Build the mechanism first. At birth, lung expansion and oxygenation lower P V R while placental separation raises S V R, changing flow across the ductus arteriosus and foramen ovale.

At the bedside, persistent high P V R or structural heart disease can preserve fetal shunt patterns and produce severe hypoxemia.

Two traps to actively avoid: Interpreting neonatal oxygenation without pre/post-ductal context. And: Assuming adult circulation physiology immediately after birth.

The integration move is this: The first minutes to days of life are a rapidly changing pressure-and-resistance experiment.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: ductus arteriosus, foramen ovale, persistent pulmonary hypertension.
