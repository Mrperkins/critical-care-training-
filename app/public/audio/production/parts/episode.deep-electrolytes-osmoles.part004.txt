Now: Hypoglycemia as a brain emergency.

Critical hypoglycemia. Hypoglycemia is a neurologic emergency whose cause may reflect insulin/drugs, organ failure, endocrine failure or severe infection.

Build the mechanism first. The brain depends heavily on circulating glucose and has limited fuel reserve, so severe hypoglycemia can rapidly cause seizure, coma and adrenergic stress.

At the bedside, correct glucose immediately while identifying ongoing insulin/drug effect, hepatic failure, adrenal disease, sepsis or nutritional depletion that can make recurrence likely.

Two traps to actively avoid: Stopping after one normal repeat glucose. And: Attributing persistent altered mental status to hypoglycemia after the glucose has been corrected without reassessment.

The integration move is this: Treat the neuroglycopenia fast, then treat the reason it happened.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: neuroglycopenia, counter-regulation.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Treat immediately while simultaneously searching for the cause. Recognize recurrent hypoglycemia as a clue to ongoing physiology or drug exposure.
