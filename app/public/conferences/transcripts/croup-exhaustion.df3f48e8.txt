The stridor gets softer as the child tires
Case conference. Life-threatening croup with exhaustion and impending complete upper-airway obstruction. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 3-year-old child, measured weight 14 kilograms, has worsening noisy breathing. The initial barking cough is followed by increasing distress. During assessment the child becomes drowsy and the stridor becomes softer. EMS is called for a barking cough and stridor that has progressed through the evening. The father reports an earlier cold, then progressively deeper chest recession. There is no abrupt choking event. Initial corticosteroid and nebulized epinephrine at the referring department give only transient benefit. The child now has less audible stridor while becoming drowsy. Severe croup is the working diagnosis; poor response obliges us to consider epiglottitis, bacterial tracheitis and a foreign body while preparing rescue. The change in sound is easy to notice; the change in the child is more important. This case begins at the point where an expected croup story becomes a threatened-airway story.

Atypical severity broadens the differential
A critically narrowed airway must be supported before diagnostic imaging. Avoid upsetting the child with unnecessary throat examination or positioning.
