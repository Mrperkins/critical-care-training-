Now: What feeding is trying to accomplish.

ICU nutrition & refeeding risk. Nutrition in critical illness is a metabolic therapy with timing, route, protein, glycemic and electrolyte consequences.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: enteral nutrition, protein target, caloric debt.

Now: When feeding itself becomes dangerous.

Refeeding syndrome. Insulin-driven intracellular shifts after refeeding can rapidly lower phosphate, potassium and magnesium and precipitate cardiopulmonary or neurologic deterioration.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: phosphate, thiamine, intracellular shift.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Identify high-risk patients before feeding escalates. Connect electrolyte shifts to respiratory, cardiac and neurologic failure.

Tie this back to the patient. Ask what variable is changing, what downstream effect you expect, and what bedside finding would support or contradict that model.

Now: Protein, mobility and recovery.

ICU-acquired weakness & recovery. Critical illness, immobility, inflammation, sedation and organ failure can produce profound weakness and long-term functional loss.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: critical illness myopathy, critical illness neuropathy, PICS.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Recognize weakness as an ICU outcome, not merely a discharge problem. Connect sedation, mobility, nutrition and ventilator liberation to recovery.

Tie this back to the patient. Ask what variable is changing, what downstream effect you expect, and what bedside finding would support or contradict that model.
