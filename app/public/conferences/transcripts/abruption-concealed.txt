Severe pain with very little visible blood
Case conference. Severe pain with very little visible blood. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 31-year-old patient is pregnant at 34 weeks and 4 days. Sudden abdominal pain is followed by reduced fetal movement. Only a small amount of dark vaginal blood is visible.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The uterus is tender and persistently firm. The patient is pale and diaphoretic with weak peripheral pulses.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Maternal heart rate is 126 per minute. Maternal pressure is 92 over 58. Fetal heart rate remains near 95 per minute. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Hemoglobin is 10.8 grams per deciliter. Fibrinogen is 165 milligrams per deciliter. Platelet count is 110 billion per liter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. The clinical pattern raises urgent concern for abruption. Blood can be concealed behind the placenta. A reassuring ultrasound does not reliably exclude the diagnosis.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Can the small visible blood loss explain away the signs of shock? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. External bleeding can underestimate total hemorrhage. Maternal resuscitation and urgent obstetric action are needed when pain, uterine findings and fetal compromise suggest abruption. Priorities. 1. Activate urgent obstetric and hemorrhage support and coordinate the fastest appropriate definitive care. 2. Resuscitate the mother with positioning, access and indicated blood products under protocol. 3. Continue maternal and fetal assessment without delaying action for a scan.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The receiving obstetric team prepares immediate intervention while the transport team supports maternal circulation. Concealed blood loss and evolving coagulation abnormalities remain central handoff concerns. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
