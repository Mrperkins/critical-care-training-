Now: What feeding is trying to accomplish.

ICU nutrition & refeeding risk. Nutrition in critical illness is a metabolic therapy with timing, route, protein, glycemic and electrolyte consequences.

Build the mechanism first. Critical illness changes protein turnover, insulin sensitivity and energy use. Nutrition cannot fully reverse catabolism but route, timing and protein strategy influence recovery.

At the bedside, prefer enteral feeding when feasible, assess aspiration/hemodynamic risk and monitor tolerance rather than chasing an exact calorie number immediately.

Two traps to actively avoid: Equating calories with adequate nutrition. And: Overfeeding unstable patients or missing refeeding risk.

The integration move is this: Nutrition, mobility and ventilator liberation are connected recovery interventions.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: enteral nutrition, protein target, caloric debt.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Explain why early enteral nutrition is often preferred when feasible. Recognize when feeding strategy, aspiration risk or hemodynamic instability changes the plan.

Now connect ICU nutrition & refeeding risk to Refeeding syndrome. Insulin-driven intracellular shifts after refeeding can rapidly lower phosphate, potassium and magnesium and precipitate cardiopulmonary or neurologic deterioration.

The relationship matters because Reintroducing carbohydrate after prolonged undernutrition raises insulin and shifts phosphate, potassium and magnesium intracellularly while increasing thiamine demand. At the bedside, identify high-risk patients before feeding escalation and monitor electrolytes and cardiopulmonary/neurologic response.

Now connect ICU nutrition & refeeding risk to ICU-acquired weakness & recovery. Critical illness, immobility, inflammation, sedation and organ failure can produce profound weakness and long-term functional loss.

The relationship matters because Inflammation, immobility, neuropathy, myopathy, sedation and undernutrition can cause profound ICU-acquired weakness and prolonged post-ICU disability. At the bedside, build mobility, delirium prevention, nutrition and sedation minimization into daily care rather than starting “rehab” only after organ failure resolves.

Stress-test the model. Imagine the patient changes in a way that makes equating calories with adequate nutrition tempting. Before acting, state the mechanism you think is operating, predict what your intervention should change, and name the finding that would make you stop and reconsider.
