Now connect Electrolytes & cardiac electrophysiology to Rhythm & conduction emergencies. Tachy- and bradyarrhythmias become critical when they impair filling, output or perfusion; treatment depends on rhythm mechanism and patient stability.

The relationship matters because Rhythm affects cardiac output through rate, filling time, AV synchrony and ventricular activation; the same rate can be tolerated or catastrophic depending on reserve. At the bedside, first ask whether the rhythm is causing instability or compensating for another problem, then identify the electrical mechanism and choose synchronization, pacing or medication accordingly.

Now: Calcium, magnesium and phosphate.

Calcium, magnesium & phosphate. Divalent electrolyte abnormalities alter cardiac conduction, muscle function, vascular tone and cellular energetics.

Build the mechanism first. Calcium, magnesium and phosphate participate in membrane stability, contraction, ATP biology and neuromuscular function, so derangements can produce arrhythmia, weakness, seizure or shock.

At the bedside, interpret ionized calcium when physiology demands it and anticipate depletion during massive transfusion, refeeding, diuresis and extracorporeal therapy.

Two traps to actively avoid: Treating total calcium without considering binding/albumin. And: Replacing one electrolyte while ignoring coupled magnesium or phosphate abnormalities.

The integration move is this: Electrolytes are interacting physiologic variables, not independent laboratory chores.
