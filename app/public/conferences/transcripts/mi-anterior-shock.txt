Chest pain followed by pulmonary edema
Case conference. Chest pain followed by pulmonary edema. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 67-year-old patient has 90 minutes of chest pain followed by severe breathlessness. The ECG shows a large anterior injury pattern. Confusion and a falling blood pressure develop while transfer is arranged.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The patient is cool, clammy and tachypneic with bilateral crackles and weak pulses. A new murmur or other mechanical complication is actively assessed.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 124 per minute. Blood pressure is 78 over 50. Respiratory rate is 32 per minute. Saturation is 86 percent on room air. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Lactate is 5.4 millimoles per liter. Creatinine is 1.7 milligrams per deciliter. High-sensitivity troponin is above the local decision cutoff. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. The synthetic ECG report shows anterior ST elevation. Bedside echocardiography demonstrates severe left-ventricular systolic dysfunction; specialist assessment also evaluates mechanical complications.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Does pulmonary edema exclude a simultaneous low-output circulation problem? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Congestion and poor forward flow can coexist. Acute infarction with shock requires immediate reperfusion planning while supporting oxygenation and circulation. Priorities. 1. Activate urgent coronary and cardiogenic-shock care with the receiving team. 2. Support oxygenation and perfusion with attention to the hemodynamic effects of respiratory interventions. 3. Evaluate mechanical complications and arrange advanced support capability when indicated.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The receiving team prepares emergency intervention. The handoff includes the ECG timeline, pressure trend, respiratory support and signs suggesting or arguing against a mechanical complication.
