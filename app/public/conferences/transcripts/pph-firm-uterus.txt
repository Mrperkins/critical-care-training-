Heavy bleeding with a firm uterus
Case conference. Heavy bleeding with a firm uterus. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 34-year-old patient is 45 minutes postpartum after an instrument-assisted birth. The patient has ongoing bleeding despite uterine measures. The uterus is now firm, and the estimated loss exceeds 1,500 milliliters.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The patient is pale and anxious. The referring team reports concern for a genital tract laceration and an incomplete placental examination.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 140 per minute. Blood pressure is 82 over 46. Temperature is 35.8 degrees Celsius. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Hemoglobin is 8.1 grams per deciliter. Fibrinogen is 145 milligrams per deciliter. Platelet count is 94 billion per liter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. A firm uterus with persistent bleeding prompts urgent evaluation for trauma, retained tissue and coagulopathy. Successful treatment of one cause does not exclude another.

What would you prioritize?

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Repeating the same atony intervention without reassessing the source can delay definitive control. Multiple causes of postpartum hemorrhage may coexist. Priorities. 1. Continue hemorrhage resuscitation and obtain immediate obstetric evaluation for source control. 2. Communicate the firm uterus, delivery instrumentation and uncertainty about placental completeness. 3. Maintain warming, blood product coordination and serial coagulation assessment under the hemorrhage pathway.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The obstetric team proceeds to definitive assessment and repair while resuscitation continues.
