Pain persists after delivery
Case conference. The laboratory results are worse than the pressure. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 26-year-old patient is 18 hours postpartum. Persistent upper abdominal pain, nausea and profound malaise continue after delivery. The blood pressure has not reached the severe range, and the initial concern is postoperative discomfort. The delivery is complete and the pressure does not look dramatic. Those two facts can make continued pain sound less urgent than it is. Let the examination and laboratory pattern have their own voice.

The examination adds a bleeding clue
The patient is pale with right upper quadrant tenderness and new petechiae around venipuncture sites. Mental status and breathing are currently normal. The petechiae are a small visible finding that connects to a much larger laboratory problem. Do not let the patient’s current ability to talk comfortably substitute for a systemic assessment.

Nonsevere pressure cannot clear organ injury
Heart rate is 108 per minute. Blood pressure is 142 over 90. Saturation is 98 percent. The pressure is information, but it is not an exclusion test for HELLP. We need to see whether the symptoms and other evidence point to significant disease despite that reading.

The laboratory pattern carries the urgency
Platelet count is 48 billion per liter. A S T is 340 units per liter. L D H is 1080 units per liter. Haptoglobin is low, supporting hemolysis in this context. These results are markedly abnormal in a way that fits the ongoing symptoms. The hemolysis evidence, liver injury and platelet count deserve urgent interpretation even without a severe pressure reading.

Postpartum HELLP and its mimics
HELLP can present postpartum and without severe hypertension. Other thrombotic microangiopathies and liver disorders must also be considered by the specialist team. There is still diagnostic work to do. The appropriate response to that uncertainty is expert evaluation and close monitoring, rather than waiting for the blood pressure to become more convincing.

Escalate on the evidence already present
The absence of severe hypertension does not negate major platelet, liver and hemolysis abnormalities. Delivery also does not guarantee immediate resolution. Obtain urgent obstetric and critical care review and trend the laboratory abnormalities. Assess for active bleeding and evolving hepatic or neurological complications. Plan monitored transfer and specialist management rather than waiting for severe hypertension to develop. The clinical risk comes from the syndrome and its trajectory. The handoff should make clear why a relatively modest pressure reading has not reduced the required level of care.

Follow what happens after the first panel
The platelet count is repeated and the clinical course is monitored closely. The handoff emphasizes the postpartum timing and the trajectory of symptoms and laboratories. Delivery does not promise an immediate end to the illness. Reassessment must be able to detect persistence or progression instead of treating postpartum status as a conclusion.
