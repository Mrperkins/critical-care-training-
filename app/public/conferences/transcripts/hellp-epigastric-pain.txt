“It feels like bad indigestion”
Case conference. “It feels like bad indigestion”. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 30-year-old patient is pregnant at 35 weeks and 1 day. Nausea and upper abdominal pain worsen through the afternoon. The patient initially attributes the symptoms to reflux. There is no external bleeding.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. Right upper quadrant tenderness and malaise are present. The patient is alert. The team is concerned about a systemic pregnancy complication rather than treating the pain as isolated indigestion.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 112 per minute. Blood pressure is 162 over 108. Saturation is 97 percent. Fetal heart rate is 155 per minute. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Platelet count is 62 billion per liter. A S T is 280 units per liter. L D H is 920 units per liter. The laboratory reports schistocytes on the blood smear. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. Hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes and low platelets support HELLP syndrome in context. Severe or escalating abdominal pain also raises concern for hepatic complications requiring urgent specialist assessment.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Which laboratory pattern connects the abdominal pain to a time-critical obstetric disorder? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. The platelet count and evidence of liver injury and hemolysis change the meaning of an apparently gastrointestinal complaint. Obstetric evaluation must accompany resuscitation. Priorities. 1. Escalate immediately to obstetric and critical care teams and coordinate definitive care. 2. Monitor for bleeding, neurological change and worsening abdominal pain; repeat laboratories as directed. 3. Treat associated severe hypertension and provide indicated seizure prophylaxis under the obstetric pathway.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The receiving team arranges urgent obstetric management. Serial symptoms and laboratory values are communicated, with preparation for hemorrhage and blood products if required. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.

HELLP syndrome: Epigastric pain and evolving laboratory injury
Case synopsis. HELLP syndrome. Epigastric pain and evolving laboratory injury. Abdominal pain became a systemic emergency when interpreted with thrombocytopenia, hemolysis and liver injury.
