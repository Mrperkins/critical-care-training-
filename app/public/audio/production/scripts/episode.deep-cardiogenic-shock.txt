Now: LV, RV and biventricular failure.

Cardiogenic shock. Pump failure where restoring perfusion must be balanced against congestion, myocardial oxygen demand, coronary perfusion and mechanical complications.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: cardiac power output, congestion, inotropy.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Phenotype left-, right- and biventricular pump failure. Choose support based on pressure, flow, congestion and the cause of pump failure.

Right ventricular failure. Failure of a thin-walled ventricle that is highly sensitive to acute afterload, volume, perfusion pressure and ventricular interaction.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: RV-PA coupling, septal shift, transmural pressure.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Recognize RV shock physiology before systemic hypotension becomes profound. Predict how peep, hypercapnia, hypoxemia, fluids and vasoactives may change RV performance.

Tie this back to the patient. Ask what variable is changing, what downstream effect you expect, and what bedside finding would support or contradict that model.

Now: Pressor versus inotrope tradeoffs.

Vasopressor & inotrope physiology. Vasoactive drugs change arterial tone, venous tone, heart rate, contractility and regional flow; the desired effect depends on the shock model.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: alpha-1, beta-1, inotropy, chronotropy, venoconstriction.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Choose a vasoactive strategy from the physiologic deficit rather than blood pressure alone. State the expected benefit, predictable tradeoff and reassessment endpoint.

Now: When drugs are no longer enough.

Mechanical circulatory support. Temporary circulatory support devices unload or bypass different parts of the circulation and must be matched to the failing ventricle and oxygenation problem.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: LV unloading, VA E C M O, axial flow.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Match support strategy to LV, RV, biventricular or cardiopulmonary failure. Anticipate loading, limb, hemolysis and device-interaction complications.
