Chest pain becomes a breathing and perfusion emergency
Case conference. Chest pain followed by pulmonary edema. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 67-year-old patient has 90 minutes of chest pain followed by severe breathlessness. The ECG shows a large anterior injury pattern. Confusion and a falling blood pressure develop while transfer is arranged. The problem has evolved beyond a chest pain complaint. The change in breathing and mental status asks us to organize urgent coronary care and shock support at the same time.

Oxygenation and circulation are both failing
Heart rate is 124 per minute. Blood pressure is 78 over 50. Respiratory rate is 32 per minute. Saturation is 86 percent on room air. The low pressure and hypoxemia are simultaneous problems. The next respiratory intervention should be planned with its possible hemodynamic effect already in mind.

Evidence of systemic stress
Lactate is 5.4 millimoles per liter. Creatinine is 1.7 milligrams per deciliter. High-sensitivity troponin is above the local decision cutoff. The laboratory findings add to the evidence of myocardial injury and impaired perfusion. They support urgency while the reperfusion and complication assessment proceeds.

Reperfuse and support the failing circulation
Congestion and poor forward flow can coexist. Acute infarction with shock requires immediate reperfusion planning while supporting oxygenation and circulation. Activate urgent coronary and cardiogenic-shock care with the receiving team. Support oxygenation and perfusion with attention to the hemodynamic effects of respiratory interventions. Evaluate mechanical complications and arrange advanced support capability when indicated. The destination must match the severity. Early communication allows the receiving team to prepare definitive intervention and the level of support the patient may need on arrival.

Prepare the receiving intervention
The receiving team prepares emergency intervention. The handoff includes the ECG timeline, pressure trend, respiratory support and signs suggesting or arguing against a mechanical complication. A useful transfer summary explains how the patient changed, not only where the ECG elevation was. It should show whether each support intervention was tolerated.
