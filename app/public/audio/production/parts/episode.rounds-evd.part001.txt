Now: The drain is a hydrostatic system.

E V D management. External ventricular drainage requires correct leveling, clamping during movement and interpretation of drainage and ICP.

Build the mechanism first. An external ventricular drain uses a hydrostatic height reference to define the pressure at which CSF drains while also allowing ICP monitoring depending on system configuration.

At the bedside, level to the ordered reference point, manage clamping during movement according to policy, and re-level after position changes before interpreting pressure or drainage.

Two traps to actively avoid: Moving the bed without reconsidering the drainage height. And: Treating drainage amount independently of chamber position and patient physiology.
