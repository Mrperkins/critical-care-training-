Headache and flashing lights at 33 weeks
Case conference. Headache and flashing lights at 33 weeks. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 28-year-old patient is pregnant at 33 weeks and 2 days, with a singleton pregnancy. Persistent headache, flashing lights and right upper quadrant discomfort develop over several hours. There is no seizure, vaginal bleeding or known chronic hypertension.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The patient is alert with brisk reflexes and mild dependent edema. The lungs are clear. Severe blood pressure readings persist when repeated with an appropriate cuff.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Maternal heart rate is 104 per minute. Blood pressure is 174 over 114 and remains severe on repeat assessment. Maternal saturation is 97 percent on room air. Fetal heart rate is 150 per minute at this assessment. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Platelet count is 118 billion per liter. A S T is 85 units per liter. Creatinine is 1.2 milligrams per deciliter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. The findings support preeclampsia with severe features. Proteinuria testing contributes to evaluation but is not required to recognize severe disease when other qualifying features are present.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Does magnesium replace an antihypertensive? Can a currently normal fetal rate justify delaying maternal stabilization? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Seizure prevention and treatment of severe blood pressure serve different purposes. Maternal stabilization protects both patients, and the fetus requires ongoing assessment rather than a single reassuring number. Priorities. 1. Begin urgent obstetric escalation and treat persistent severe hypertension under the obstetric protocol. 2. Give indicated magnesium for seizure prophylaxis and monitor breathing, reflexes, renal function and infusion delivery. 3. Coordinate maternal and fetal monitoring and the specialist plan for delivery after stabilization.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. Blood pressure improves under treatment and headache lessens, but the patient still requires specialist obstetric management. The handoff includes repeat pressures, symptoms, laboratory trends and exact medication times. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
