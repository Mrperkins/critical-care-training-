Cold extremities after delivery
Case conference. Cold extremities after delivery. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 36-year-old patient is ten days postpartum. Rapidly worsening dyspnea is accompanied by confusion and scant urine. The patient has received a small fluid intervention with no improvement.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The patient is cool and clammy with weak pulses, crackles and elevated neck veins. The team recognizes simultaneous congestion and poor perfusion.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 138 per minute. Blood pressure is 78 over 48. Respiratory rate is 34 per minute. Saturation is 88 percent on supplemental oxygen. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Lactate is 5.8 millimoles per liter. Creatinine is 1.8 milligrams per deciliter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. Bedside echocardiography shows severe global left-ventricular dysfunction. The differential includes peripartum cardiomyopathy and other causes of acute heart failure or shock. A single echocardiographic finding does not replace etiological evaluation. In visual mode, an optional real reference image can be opened. It belongs to a different patient and is not the scan from this fictional case.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Would more fluid necessarily improve the pressure in a patient who is both congested and poorly perfused? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Cardiogenic shock requires support of forward flow while respecting pulmonary congestion. Respiratory interventions can also alter hemodynamics, so escalation needs careful planning. Priorities. 1. Activate cardiogenic-shock and obstetric consultation with a destination capable of advanced circulatory support. 2. Support oxygenation and perfusion under specialist guidance; reassess tolerance of each intervention. 3. Communicate the postpartum timing and evaluate alternative causes including ischemia and embolic disease.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. The receiving team prepares intensive care and possible mechanical support while transfer stabilization continues. The handoff includes urine output, oxygen requirement and response to each intervention. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
