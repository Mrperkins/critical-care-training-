The bleeding continues after birth
Case conference. The bleeding continues after birth. This is a fictional teaching case, with clinical review pending. A 29-year-old patient is 20 minutes postpartum after vaginal delivery at term. Measured blood loss is approximately 1,200 milliliters and rising. The placenta has been delivered. The patient feels faint and nauseated.

What do you notice?
At the bedside. The uterus is boggy and enlarged, with ongoing brisk bleeding. The patient is pale with cool extremities. The team evaluates tone, trauma, tissue and coagulation causes together.

Vital signs
The initial vital signs are as follows. Heart rate is 132 per minute. Blood pressure is 88 over 52. Respiratory rate is 28 per minute. Saturation is 97 percent. These are observations at this point in the case, rather than treatment targets.

Laboratory findings
Laboratory findings. Early hemoglobin is 10.4 grams per deciliter; this does not exclude major acute blood loss. Fibrinogen is 190 milligrams per deciliter. Lactate is 4.9 millimoles per liter. Interpret these results alongside the examination and the timing of sampling.

Diagnostic findings
Diagnostic findings. The bedside finding of a boggy uterus supports atony, while continued assessment looks for additional causes. Imaging must not delay hemorrhage control and resuscitation.

What would you prioritize?
Think it through. Would you wait for hemoglobin to fall before activating a hemorrhage response? Consider your answer while the case continues. No screen interaction is needed.

Clinical reasoning & priorities
Conference discussion. Acute bleeding can initially leave a deceptively preserved hemoglobin. The observed blood loss and shock require action, and a relatively low fibrinogen is concerning in obstetric hemorrhage. Priorities. 1. Activate the obstetric hemorrhage pathway, including source control, uterine measures and indicated uterotonics. 2. Coordinate blood products and timely tranexamic acid when indicated, with warming and monitoring under protocol. 3. Quantify ongoing loss and escalate promptly for mechanical or surgical control if bleeding continues.

Reassessment & handoff
Reassessment and handoff. Bleeding slows after treatment but the team continues quantification and perfusion monitoring. Total blood loss, uterotonic times, blood products and coagulation results accompany the handoff. This is an authored fictional course; improvement is not guaranteed in a real patient.
