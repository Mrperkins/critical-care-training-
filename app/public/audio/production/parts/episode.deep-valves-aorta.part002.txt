Now: Dissection and aneurysmal catastrophe.

Aortic dissection & aneurysmal catastrophe. Acute aortic syndromes combine vascular-wall failure with risks of rupture, malperfusion, tamponade and acute valvular dysfunction.

Build the mechanism first. Aortic wall disruption or aneurysmal rupture can cause hemorrhage, branch-vessel malperfusion, acute aortic regurgitation or tamponade.

At the bedside, reduce shear/impulse when appropriate while preserving organ perfusion and accelerating definitive surgical/endovascular evaluation.

Two traps to actively avoid: Lowering blood pressure without controlling tachycardia/shear in dissection. And: Missing tamponade or malperfusion because chest pain has improved.

The integration move is this: The aorta can fail as both a conduit and a source of catastrophic secondary organ ischemia.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: impulse control, malperfusion, rupture.
