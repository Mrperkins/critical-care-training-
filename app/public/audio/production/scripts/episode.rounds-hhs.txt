Now: Why HHS is not DKA without ketones.

Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state. HHS combines profound free-water deficit and hyperosmolality with less ketogenesis than DKA, creating distinct neurologic and correction-rate concerns.

Build the mechanism first. HHS is dominated by severe hyperosmolality and free-water deficit with relatively little ketoacidosis. Neurologic dysfunction tracks osmotic disturbance and underlying illness.

At the bedside, follow effective osmolality, sodium trajectory, volume status and neurologic state while glucose falls.

Two traps to actively avoid: Treating HHS as DKA with fewer ketones. And: Correcting glucose rapidly without watching osmolality and sodium.

The integration move is this: The treatment target is gradual restoration of water and osmolality, not merely glucose normalization.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: effective osmolality, free-water deficit, corrected sodium.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Distinguish HHS physiology from DKA rather than treating both as hyperglycemia. Track sodium and osmolality during therapy to avoid overly rapid shifts.

Clinical uncertainty & model revision. Expert reasoning explicitly tracks uncertainty, contradictory data and what finding would falsify the current working model.

Build the mechanism first. Clinical decisions are made with incomplete information. Calibrated reasoning assigns provisional probability, makes predictions and updates when new data contradict the model.

At the bedside, state confidence, name disconfirming evidence and define what response you expect from a treatment if the model is correct.

Two traps to actively avoid: Equating uncertainty with indecision. And: Becoming more confident simply because the same hypothesis has been repeated.

The integration move is this: A model that cannot be falsified is not useful clinical reasoning.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: calibration, falsification, diagnostic time-out.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: State confidence, identify disconfirming evidence and update the model when the patient fails to respond as predicted.
