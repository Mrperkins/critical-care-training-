At the bedside, before intervening, name the intended benefit, the organ/system at risk and the signal that would tell you the tradeoff has become unfavorable.

Two traps to actively avoid: Optimizing one organ in isolation. And: Treating guideline targets as independent goals that can all be maximized simultaneously.

The integration move is this: Expert critical care is often choosing the least harmful global strategy rather than a perfect organ-specific intervention.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Explain at least two competing organ effects of a proposed intervention. Choose the least harmful testable strategy when the optimal answer is uncertain.

The relationship matters because Clinical decisions are made with incomplete information. Calibrated reasoning assigns provisional probability, makes predictions and updates when new data contradict the model. At the bedside, state confidence, name disconfirming evidence and define what response you expect from a treatment if the model is correct.
