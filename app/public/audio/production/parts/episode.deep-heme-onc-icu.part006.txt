Two traps to actively avoid: Equating INR with whole-blood clot quality. And: Ignoring fibrinogen, platelet function, temperature and calcium.

The integration move is this: Coagulation is a dynamic system, not a single laboratory pathway.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: fibrinolysis, fibrinogen, viscoelastic testing.

What expert-level use of this concept looks like is this: Reason from the failing component of clot formation rather than treating INR alone. Anticipate temperature, calcium and dilution effects during hemorrhagic resuscitation.

Now connect Critical-illness coagulopathy to Massive transfusion & hemorrhagic resuscitation. Hemorrhagic resuscitation integrates source control, balanced component replacement, calcium, temperature, coagulation and perfusion.

The relationship matters because Major hemorrhage produces loss of oxygen-carrying capacity, volume and hemostatic components while hypothermia, hypocalcemia, dilution and acidosis can worsen coagulation. At the bedside, activate the hemorrhage system early, warm the resuscitation, track calcium/coagulation/temperature and never lose sight of definitive source control.
