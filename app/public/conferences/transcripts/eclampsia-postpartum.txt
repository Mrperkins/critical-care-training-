Postpartum eclampsia remains possible
Postpartum timing does not exclude eclampsia. Atypical features, focal deficits or persistent altered mental status increase the need for urgent neurological evaluation and imaging after stabilization. The diagnosis is approached through timing and clinical features, while other causes are evaluated. Persistent altered consciousness or focal findings should change how urgently we pursue additional neurological assessment.

Support breathing, treat seizure risk and pressure
Eclampsia can occur postpartum and without prior recognition of preeclampsia. Simultaneous evaluation of alternative seizure causes prevents premature closure. Protect airway and ventilation, prevent injury and treat suspected eclampsia with magnesium under protocol. Treat persistent severe hypertension and arrange immediate obstetric consultation. Reassess neurological recovery and pursue urgent imaging or additional diagnostics when indicated. Parallel care is the key. We can protect breathing, treat the suspected obstetric syndrome and plan further diagnostics without forcing those tasks into an unnecessarily slow sequence.
