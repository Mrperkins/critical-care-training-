Source control. Definitive management of infected material, devitalized tissue or an infected device can be as important as antimicrobial therapy.

Build the mechanism first. Antibiotics cannot sterilize every infected collection, necrotic tissue or infected device. Persistent nidus can sustain inflammatory and microbial burden despite apparently appropriate antimicrobial therapy.

At the bedside, ask early whether drainage, debridement, device removal or surgery is required and whether it is actually happening.

Two traps to actively avoid: Escalating antibiotic breadth when the real failure is lack of source control. And: Waiting for complete hemodynamic normalization before addressing a time-sensitive source.

The integration move is this: Sepsis treatment is host support plus antimicrobials plus definitive control of the source.

Keep these terms available in your mental model: drainage, debridement, device removal.
